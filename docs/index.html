About

A scored challenge run for MHGU. It doesn’t roll quests and it has no filters — you hunt whatever you like, then come back and say what happened. What it tracks is what that costs you.

What’s at stake

Your stock is every weapon and style pairing — 14 weapons across 6 styles, 84 combos. When a hunt goes badly on the terms you set, the combo you were using is struck off for the rest of the run.

Combos cost nothing to own, so nothing you ground for is ever at risk. What a run takes is your options, and it takes the comfortable ones first, because those are the ones you reach for.

How far it goes

A run is 50 successful hunts, or however far you get before nothing is left standing. Only clears count towards the 50 — a failure costs you a combo, it doesn’t spend your run.

Clearing a quest spends it. Fifty clears means fifty different quests, so a run is a route through the quest list rather than the same hunt fifty times. Failing leaves it on the board, which is what lets the quest lock send you back to it.

What you’re playing for

Clearing a quest earns its real in-game zenny reward, and every combo still standing at the end pays a bonus on top. Harsher rules multiply what you earn, so the score is a statement about how you played rather than how long you lasted — a careful run under gentle rules and a brutal one cut short can land in the same place.

Not part of a run

Arena quests. They hand you a fixed set of five weapons, so there is no combo of yours to put at stake — and a rule that ties you to one combo could not be honoured on them anyway. They aren’t listed.

Prowler quests. You hunt those as a Palico, so again none of your 84 combos is on the line. Also not listed.

Where it came from

This started as a Nuzlocke-inspired challenge idea and ended up rather less traditional than one — out of curiosity about how to balance different levels of difficulty against each other.

How it works

Each hunt

  1. Get a combo. Either the app hands you a surviving weapon and style, or you pick one yourself. Fallen combos are never offered.
  2. Name the quest you’re about to hunt. Named the wrong one? The × on the card clears it — nothing is committed until you report a result.
  3. Report the result — Cleared, Carted, or Failed. Carted can be pressed more than once; it doesn’t end the attempt.

The kill condition you chose is checked against that result. When it fires, the combo you were using is struck off the board.

Reading the board

The grid is every weapon against every style. Each row is its weapon’s colour, and each cell is tagged with its style — G, S and V for Guild, Striker and Valor, Ad, Ae and Al for Adept, Aerial and Alchemy.

  • The weapon’s icon — available, you can be handed it or pick it.
  • A bone on charcoal — fallen. Gone for the rest of the run.
  • Darkened and empty — the weapon retired, so this one is non-selectable. It was never lost: the weapon spent its style allowance and took its survivors out of the pool with it.

The combo you’re on is found by the crosshair — a blue band across its row, a red band down its column, and a purple box where they cross. The row label and the style header light with them.

The name at the start of each row says what has happened to that weapon: Selected while you’re on one of its styles, and Retired once it’s out of the pool.

With revives on, a fallen combo can be clicked to buy it back.

Reading the result

The result screen shows three things side by side. Rules is the set of choices the run was played under, kept as it was when you pressed Start Run — changing the settings afterwards won’t rewrite it. Quest Log lists every combo that fell, in order, with the quest it fell on.

In the middle is your score: everything you earned, plus the bonus for combos still standing, with the difficulty you played at. Below that is how the run actually went — hunts taken, clears, failures, carts, and any rerolls or revives you paid for.

Once a run is over you can move between the Board and the Result with the tabs above them, and the last run’s result stays reachable after the next one starts.

Reading the difficulty

Every setting carries a size — XS through XL, or ±0 for the baseline. A + pays more than the baseline and a − pays less; the letter is how much. They light up when the option they sit on is the one in play, and the box at the top of the sidebar totals them into a rating from Very Easy to Very Hard.

The settings

Only one kill condition is ever in play. Carting and failing a quest are separate things — you can time out or slay a capture target without carting, and you can cart twice and still clear — so the harshest setting covers both explicitly. Below that, each rule already covers the gentler ones beneath it.

How long you hold a weapon/style. Until it falls is the baseline: you keep it through clears as well as failures, until it dies. Until you clear a quest hands it in every time you succeed, but a failure keeps it, so you can take the quest that beat you again with the same combo. Changes each quest is harsher still, because it hands the combo in win or lose — you never get to keep one, not even for the retry you are owed. Both of those pay more than the baseline.

The quest lock means a quest that beat you is owed one retry — your next hunt has to be that quest, win or lose. It’s on by default; turning it off makes the run easier and pays less.

Revives and rerolls are both off by default. A revive buys a fallen combo back; a reroll refuses the combo you were handed and draws another. Both come out of your earned zenny, and both double in price each time you use them — so they’re there to rescue a run, not to run on.

The settings are frozen for the length of a run and unlock once it ends.
