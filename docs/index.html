How it works

This tracks a scored challenge run. It doesn't roll quests — hunt whatever you like, then come back and say what happened.

  1. Get a combo. Either the app hands you a surviving weapon and style, or you pick one yourself. Fallen combos are never offered.
  2. Name the quest you're about to hunt.
  3. Report the result — Cleared, Carted, or Failed. Carted can be pressed more than once; it doesn't end the attempt.

The kill condition you chose is checked against that result. When it fires, the weapon/style combo you were using is struck off the board permanently. There are 92 of them — 14 weapons across 6 styles, plus a Prowler with 8 biases.

The run

A run is 50 successful hunts, or however far you get before nothing is left standing. Only clears count towards the 50 — a failure costs you a combo, it doesn't spend your run.

Clear a quest and it's spent. Fifty clears means fifty different quests, so a run is a route rather than the same quest fifty times. Only clearing spends one; fail and it stays on the board, which is what lets the quest lock send you back to it.

Arena quests aren't part of a run. They come with fixed sets. Some conditions force you to use a weapon/style combo, and Arena would interfere with that — the app would also have to make sure you can use at least one of the five sets offered before you could enter the quest. They were dropped since people tend to care more about non-Arena quests.

Clearing a quest earns its real in-game zenny reward. Combos you're still holding at the end pay a bonus on top of what you earned.

When the run ends

The result screen shows three things side by side. Rules is the set of choices the run was played under, kept as it was when you pressed Start Run — changing the settings afterwards won't rewrite it. Quest Log lists every combo that fell, in order, with the quest it fell on.

In the middle is your score: everything you earned, plus the bonus for combos still standing, with the difficulty you played at. Below that is how the run actually went — hunts taken, clears, failures, carts, and any rerolls or revives you paid for.

Difficulty and bonus

Each choice you make impacts the Difficulty — Very Easy to Very Hard. Some choices impact the difficulty greatly while others impact it less. Every setting shows a size value — XS through XL. This is the size of the bonus you receive by selecting the option. They will light up when they are active in the selection. More difficult runs offer higher bonuses but make failing more likely.

The settings

Only one kill condition is ever in play. Carting and failing a quest are separate things — you can time out or slay a capture target without carting, and you can cart twice and still clear — so the harshest setting covers both explicitly. Below that, each rule already covers the gentler ones beneath it.

How long you hold a weapon/style. Until it falls is the baseline: you keep it through clears as well as failures, until it dies. Until you clear hands it in every time you succeed, but a failure keeps it, so you can take the quest that beat you again with the same combo. That's harder than the baseline and is the one setting that pays more than it. Swap whenever you like is the easy option.

The quest lock means a quest that beat you is owed one retry — your next hunt has to be that quest, win or lose. It's on by default; turning it off makes the run easier and pays less.

Revives and rerolls are both off by default. A revive buys a fallen combo back; a reroll refuses the combo you were handed and draws another. Both come out of your earned zenny, and both double in price each time you use them — so they're there to rescue a run, not to run on.

About

This started as a Nuzlocke-inspired challenge idea, and ended up rather less traditional than one — out of curiosity about how to balance different levels of difficulty against each other.
